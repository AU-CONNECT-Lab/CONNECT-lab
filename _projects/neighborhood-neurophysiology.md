This line of research examines how neighborhood-level stressors — including poverty, 
violence exposure, and resource scarcity — shape prefrontal cortex functioning during 
adolescence. We use fNIRS to capture real-time hemodynamic responses during social and 
cognitive tasks, linking place-based context to neural outcomes.

We are particularly interested in understanding whether and how chronic exposure to 
neighborhood disadvantage alters the sensitivity of the prefrontal cortex to 
social-evaluative stress, and what protective factors (family warmth, social support) 
buffer this association.
