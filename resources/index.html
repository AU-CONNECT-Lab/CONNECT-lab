Our method

We study two brains at once

The CONNECT Lab uses fNIRS hyperscanning — recording brain activity from two people simultaneously while they talk, play and work together. It lets us ask what happens between brains during real social interaction, not just inside one.

What is hyperscanning?

A technique that simultaneously records brain activity from two or more people during natural social interaction. Traditional neuroimaging studies people one at a time. Hyperscanning makes the pair the unit of analysis — we ask how two brains change together, second by second, while something social is actually happening between them.

From one brain to two

Both partners wear caps and are recorded on one shared clock, so their activity can be lined up moment by moment.

Real interaction

Pairs can talk, cooperate, teach or play face to face — conversation, eye contact, even drawing together.

Inter-brain synchrony

We ask whether the two brains become temporally aligned during interaction, and which partner leads.

Why this needs fNIRS

Everything that makes a social interaction worth studying — sitting up, facing a partner, talking, gesturing, moving — is exactly what an MRI scanner cannot allow. Participants lie flat and still in a loud, narrow bore, and a few millimeters of head movement is enough to distort the data.

fNIRS keeps the brain signal and changes the machine. The equipment is a soft cap, so two people can sit at a table together and simply behave, while we record both of them.

The method behind it

So what is fNIRS?

Functional near-infrared spectroscopy measures brain activity using light rather than a magnet. The short version:

It measures blood oxygen

When a region of the brain becomes active, blood flow there increases and brings more oxygen than is consumed. fNIRS tracks that change — the same physiological signal fMRI measures.

It uses harmless light

Near-infrared light passes through the scalp and skull to reach the outer layer of the brain. Oxygenated and deoxygenated hemoglobin absorb it differently, which is what we read.

It's wearable and quiet

The sensors sit in a cap. Participants can sit upright, speak, move and interact — and sessions can run in schools and community settings, not only in a lab.

It's completely safe

Low-power, non-ionizing light. No magnetic field, no radiation, no injection, no noise. Safe for repeated use, including with children and infants.

fNIRS and fMRI, briefly

Both measure the same underlying response. They differ in what they can reach and what they let a participant do — which is why we use each for different questions.

 fMRIfNIRS
CoverageWhole brain, including deep structuresCortex only
Spatial detailHigherLower (improved with high-density systems)
SettingLying flat and still in a fixed scannerSeated, standing or moving
Social interactionVery limitedFace-to-face conversation and play
Two people at onceRequires two linked scannersOne system can cap both partners

fMRI wins on depth and spatial resolution. fNIRS wins on movement tolerance, setting, and the ability to record an interaction as it happens.

One thing we're careful about

Two people doing the same task at the same time will look coupled, simply because the task is driving both brains. So every synchrony result is compared against a null — built by scrambling one partner's signal, or by pairing people who never interacted. Only the excess above that null counts as genuine interpersonal synchrony.
