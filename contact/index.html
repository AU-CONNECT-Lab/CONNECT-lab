Get Involved

Join the CONNECT Lab

We are a new lab, and we are building the team now. That makes this an unusually good moment to join — there is real work to do, and room to shape how the lab runs.

Undergraduate research assistants

Are you an Auburn undergraduate? We are actively recruiting.

If you are curious about how young people's social worlds — families, friendships, neighborhoods — shape the developing brain and body, we would like to hear from you. No prior research experience is required. What matters is curiosity, reliability, and a willingness to learn.

Because the lab is new, research assistants here do real work from the start. You will be trained on our equipment and take part in running studies, not only entering data.

What you would do

  • Train on fNIRS and help run hyperscanning sessions with participants
  • Assist with recruitment, scheduling, and data collection
  • Conduct literature reviews and learn how a study gets designed
  • Help build the lab's culture and day-to-day practices
  • Develop an independent project, if that is something you want

When to apply

Dr. Cai accepts applications for the spring and summer semesters. Getting in touch a few weeks before the semester begins gives us the best chance of finding a role that fits your schedule.

How to apply

Email Dr. Cai at {{ site.lab.pi_email }}. A short note is plenty — please include:

  • Your year and major
  • What interests you about this research
  • Roughly how many hours a week you could commit, and for how many semesters
  • A résumé or unofficial transcript, if you have one handy
Email Dr. Cai

Prospective graduate students

If you are thinking about applying to work with Dr. Cai, please email her directly before you submit an application. Graduate admissions depend a great deal on fit between your interests and where the lab is heading, and a short exchange early on tells both of us far more than an application file does.

In your email, say a little about your research interests, what draws you to this lab in particular, and any research experience you have. A CV is helpful. Questions about the program, funding, or what the next few years would actually look like are all welcome — ask them early rather than wondering.

Graduate students join through the Department of Human Development and Family Science at Auburn University.
